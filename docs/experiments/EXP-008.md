## Question

On the *stochastic, discrete* network-SIR (the model family whose AD we validated
against Quera-Bofarull 2025), two questions of the local GGN geometry:

- **C14** — is the important (stiff) eigenspace **robust to the chosen surrogate
  gradient**? Compare the GGN under Gumbel-Sigmoid vs straight-through — the two
  discrete-randomness estimators our repo shares with Quera-Bofarull 2025
  (§3.4.2 / §3.4.1); SPA/StochasticAD (§3.4.3) deferred per DEC-011.
- **C15** — does **truncated differentiation** (`grad_horizon`) destroy that
  geometry?

## Method

Model: `models/network_sir.py`, P=5. Analysed at **two operating points** to
separate a property of the geometry from a property of one regime:
- **OP_A (near-threshold):** θ\* = (β 0.03, γ 0.06, I0_frac 0.03, t_lock 0.20,
  f_lock 0.30), ER graph N=150, ⟨k⟩=6, `graph_seed=17`, T=40. Attack rate ≈29%,
  peak ≈2.3.
- **OP_B (supercritical, denser, different graph):** θ\* = (β 0.045, γ 0.10,
  I0_frac 0.03, t_lock 0.15, f_lock 0.25), ⟨k⟩=8, `graph_seed=29`, T=40. Attack
  rate ≈52%, peak ≈5.5.

Both are partial epidemics where all five parameters shape the incidence curve and
the GGN is full-rank (5 nonzero eigenvalues).

Representation: MMD mean embedding `m(z) = E_ξ[φ_RFF(incidence(z,ξ))]`, frozen RFF
(D=128, median-heuristic bandwidth 4.73, DEC-006), prior-whitened coords z∼N(0,I)
(DEC-003). GGN `G = J_mᵀ J_m` via the plug-in mean-Jacobian estimator (EXP-003 Ĝ_V),
M=128 seeds with common random numbers, **float64**. Cross-surrogate agreement =
principal angles of leading-k eigenspaces; horizon sweep on the smooth (Gumbel)
surrogate, grad_horizon ∈ {40,20,10,5}, angle vs the full-horizon reference.

FD validation (Quera-Bofarull 2025 §5.3.1 protocol): each surrogate's mean-embedding
Jacobian vs a central finite-difference reference on the **seed-averaged** embedding
with common random numbers (step 1e-2).

## Result

Cross-point summary (the robustness check):

| metric | OP_A (attack 29%, ⟨k⟩6) | OP_B (attack 52%, ⟨k⟩8, diff. graph) |
|---|---|---|
| Gumbel FD median rel-Jac error | **3.0e-5** ✓ | **3.4e-5** ✓ |
| C14 leading angle Gumbel↔ST (k=1/2/3) | 8.6° / **8.9°** / 5.9° | 8.3° / **8.4°** / 11.1° |
| C14 eigenvalue ratio ST/Gumbel | 0.22–0.43× | 0.25–0.48× |
| C15 leading angle @ half horizon (top-λ ratio) | 80.3° (0.15) | 80.8° (0.08) |
| C15 leading angle @ T/8 (top-λ ratio) | 81.7° (0.014) | 81.3° (0.004) |

- **C14 — leading eigenspace robust across surrogates, at BOTH operating points.**
  The Gumbel↔straight-through leading-2 angle is **8.9° (OP_A)** and **8.4° (OP_B)** —
  nearly identical despite a very different regime (29% vs 52% attack rate), a denser
  graph, and a different graph realization. So the agreement is a property of the
  geometry, not of the near-threshold point. The *directions* the diagnostic calls
  stiff/sloppy are surrogate-invariant; the *scale* is not (ST eigenvalues 0.22–0.48×
  the Gumbel ones at both points). One honest nuance: the third (deeper) direction is
  more surrogate-sensitive in the supercritical regime (k=3 angle 11.1° at OP_B vs
  5.9° at OP_A) — robustness is strongest for the leading stiff directions.
- **Gumbel AD is finite-difference-validated at both points** (median rel-Jac error
  3.0e-5 / 3.4e-5): the discrete-SIR AD GGN construction is correct on the smooth
  surrogate. The straight-through Jacobian does **not** match FD (1.20 / 0.88) —
  expected: its per-path forward is piecewise-constant in z, so the FD reference on
  the seed-averaged embedding is high-variance at M=128, and ST is a known-biased
  estimator. So the C14 agreement means "a biased surrogate reproduces the
  FD-validated leading directions," not that ST is itself FD-verifiable here.
- **C15 — horizon truncation destroys the geometry, at both points.** Leading-2
  eigenspace angle vs the full-horizon GGN, with top-eigenvalue ratio: at OP_A
  gh=40 **0°** (1.00) → gh=20 **80.3°** (0.15) → gh=5 **81.7°** (0.014); OP_B is the
  same picture with an even sharper eigenvalue collapse (0.4% of full at gh=5).
  Truncating differentiation to trailing steps — past the epidemic's informative
  growth phase — both rotates the stiff subspace ~80° and collapses its eigenvalue.
  The simultaneous angle-blowup **and** eigenvalue-collapse is genuine destruction of
  the local information geometry, not a mere rotation. Direct substantiation of RQ4
  (DEC-011): pathwise-differentiated geometry is only meaningful over a horizon that
  covers the informative dynamics.

## Claim impact

- **C14 → supported conditionally:** on stochastic discrete network-SIR the leading
  GGN eigenspace is robust to the surrogate gradient at **two** operating points
  (Gumbel↔straight-through leading-2 angle 8.9° / 8.4°), with the Gumbel construction
  FD-validated at both (3.0e-5 / 3.4e-5); the eigenvalue *scale* is surrogate-
  dependent (0.22–0.48×) and the deeper k=3 direction is more surrogate-sensitive
  (11° at OP_B). Scope: these two θ\*/graphs, D=128 RFF/median bandwidth, M=128,
  Gumbel-vs-ST only (SPA deferred, DEC-011); ST not FD-verifiable at this M.
- **C15 → supported conditionally:** truncated differentiation destroys the local
  information geometry at both points — halving the horizon rotates the leading
  eigenspace ~80° and collapses its curvature to 8–15% of full; shorter horizons to
  ~0.4–1%. Scope: transient epidemics; Gumbel surrogate.
- Both remain **conditional** — two operating points on one model family, RFF/MMD
  representation, SPA estimator not yet included.
