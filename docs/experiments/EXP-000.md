## Question

What did the historical Brock–Hommes OPG measure? Compare `H`, `G_MMD`, `F_OPG`,
and `C_g` at matched points with common random numbers.

## Method

BH in prior-scaled `z`; finite-feature (RFF) mean-embedding for `G`/`H`; historical
U-statistic MMD² per-seed gradients for `F_OPG`/`C_g`. Three points, residual sweep
along the stiff direction, `B=15`, float64. (Also renamed the historical OPG:
`diagnostic.scalar_gradient_opg` + `scalar_gradient_covariance`, DEC-001.)

## Result — and why it is flawed

Findings as run: BH representation looked **rank-1**; `F_OPG` coincided with `G`'s
single direction only as a rank-1 coincidence; `H` vs `G` diverged with residual.

**These conclusions are artifacts of the wrong regime.** The points used **β=2–5**,
which is the trivial near-fundamental Brock–Hommes regime (fractions near-uniform,
strategy switching inactive, trajectory ≈ noise around x=0). The model is correct;
its rich/chaotic dynamics require **large β (~50–120)** — trajectory std grows
`0.05→0.62` as β goes `2→100`. Verified against the literature
(`papers/reference_brock_hommes_1998.md`,
`papers/reference_quera_bofarull_2023_differentiable_bh.md`), 2026-07-12.
